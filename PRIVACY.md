# Privacy policy

_Tiny Courier (Android app `com.manpreetgugga.tinycourier`). Last updated 1 November 2026._

Tiny Courier has no accounts, and the developer never collects your name, email address, contacts or anything else that says who you are. The game is entirely inside the app and plays offline. From the second world on, it shows ads from **Google AdMob**, which uses the internet and is run by Google, as described below. The first world has no ads at all, and the ads aren't even started until you reach the second.

## Ads

From the second world on, the game shows two kinds of ad, and no banners:

- a **short video when you ask for one**: the first time you tap Hint in a level, the game offers a video that turns hints on for the rest of that level. You can say no;
- every 3 or 4 levels you finish, at most, a **short full-screen ad** as you move on from the level-complete screen. Never in the middle of a level, after a restart or straight after another ad.

The ads are served by Google AdMob, limited to content suitable for general audiences with parental guidance (rated PG). AdMob collects:

- your device's **advertising ID** and other device identifiers (such as the app set ID);
- your **IP address**, from which an approximate location can be worked out;
- information about the ads you see and tap, and how the app and ads perform, including crash and diagnostic data;
- basic device information, such as the model, operating system and language.

Google uses this to show ads, to measure them, to prevent fraud and, if you agree, to personalize the ads you see. Google's own policy explains how: <https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>.

- **Your choice.** Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the game asks for your consent with Google's consent form before any ad loads. You can change your answer at any time in the game's **Settings → Ad privacy**.
- **Advertising ID.** You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (**Settings → Google → Ads** on most Android phones, or **Settings → Privacy → Ads**). Without it, ads are not personalized.

## What the app keeps, and where

- **Your progress and settings** (the stars and best moves for each level, a level you're part-way through, and your settings), and a count of the levels finished since the last full-screen ad with the time of the last ad (so the game can keep ads rare), are saved on your device only. Nothing about your play is sent to the developer.
- The game's save is left out of Android's device backups.
- Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes all of it.

## Permissions

- **Vibration**, for short ticks when you tap, a mechanism clicks into place or you finish a level (you can turn it off in Settings).
- **Internet and network state**, for the ads.
- **Advertising ID**, for the ads, as described above.
- Google's ads library also declares a few permissions of its own, which the game itself doesn't use: Android's ad services (the Privacy Sandbox's privacy-preserving ad features), local network access, and keeping a short background task running.

## Children

The game is not directed at children under 13, and the developer does not knowingly collect information from anyone.
