# Privacy policy

_Tiny Courier (Android app `com.manpreetgugga.tinycourier`). Last updated 11 November 2026._

Tiny Courier has no accounts, and the developer never collects your name, email address, contacts or anything else that says who you are. The game is entirely inside the app and plays offline. It uses the internet for two things, both run by Google and described below: **ads** from Google AdMob, from the 21st level on, and **statistics and crash reports** through Google's Firebase, which you can turn off. The first 20 levels have no ads at all, and the ads aren't even started until you reach the 21st.

## Ads

From the 21st level on, the game shows two kinds of ad, and no banners:

- a **short video when you ask for one**: the first time you tap Hint in a level, the game offers a video that turns hints on for the rest of that level. You can say no;
- every 3 or 4 levels you finish, at most, a **short full-screen ad** as you move on from the level-complete screen. Never in the middle of a level, after a restart, straight after another ad or as soon as you open the game.

The ads are served by Google AdMob, limited to content suitable for general audiences with parental guidance (rated PG). AdMob collects:

- your device's **advertising ID** and other device identifiers (such as the app set ID);
- your **IP address**, from which an approximate location can be worked out;
- information about the ads you see and tap, and how the app and ads perform, including crash and diagnostic data;
- basic device information, such as the model, operating system and language.

Google uses this to show ads, to measure them, to prevent fraud and, if you agree, to personalize the ads you see. Google's own policy explains how: <https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites>.

- **Your choice.** Where the law requires it (for example in the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland), the game asks for your consent with Google's consent form before any ad loads. You can change your answer at any time in the game's **Settings → Ad privacy**.
- **Advertising ID.** You can reset or delete your advertising ID in your phone's settings (**Settings → Google → Ads** on most Android phones, or **Settings → Privacy → Ads**). Without it, ads are not personalized.

## Statistics and crash reports

On Android, the game sends the developer statistics on how it is played and reports of crashes, through two Google services that are part of Firebase. They show which levels are too hard or too easy and where the game goes wrong, so that bugs can be fixed and the levels tuned.

**Google Analytics for Firebase** records:

- each go at a level: which level, whether you finished it, your moves, stars and the seconds played, and how many hints, undos and restarts you used;
- the screens you open, the ads shown and tapped, whether you watched the video offered for hints, and how many reminders were shown and tapped;
- your settings (the graphics quality, and whether music, sounds, haptics, tilt-shift and reminders are on) and how far you've got (the levels finished and the world you're in);
- what Google Analytics collects by itself: when you open the game and how long you play, the phone's model, Android version and language, the game's version, where it was installed from, a rough location (country and region) that Google works out from your IP address without keeping the address, and a random ID for this installation of the game;
- outside the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, your device's advertising ID too (see [Ads](#ads) for how to reset or delete it).

**Firebase Crashlytics** sends a report when the game crashes, and when it runs into an error it gets past (a few each time you play, at most): what went wrong and where in the game's code, the screen and level you were on, the graphics quality, the phone's model, Android version, free memory and storage, and a random ID for this installation.

The developer sees this only as reports and totals in Google's Firebase console. Nothing in it names you, it is never sold, and it is never used to personalize ads. Google processes it for the developer as described at <https://firebase.google.com/support/privacy>, and keeps the detailed statistics for at most 14 months and crash reports for 90 days.

- **Your choice.** In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland (as your phone's SIM, network, language or time zone suggests), the game asks once, on the main menu, and sends nothing unless you tap **Share**. Elsewhere statistics are on from the start. Wherever you are, you can turn them off at any time in the game's **Settings → Statistics**: nothing more is sent, and crash reports not yet sent are deleted.

## Reminders

On Android, the game can show a short reminder notification in the morning, the afternoon and the evening. The reminders are set up and shown on your device by the game itself and need no internet. They stay quiet while you play and for a while after, and stop if you haven't opened the game for a week. When statistics are on, the game counts how many reminders are shown and how many are tapped (see above); nothing else about them leaves your device. Turn them off at any time in the game's **Settings → Reminders**, or in Android's notification settings for the game.

## What the app keeps, and where

- **Your progress and settings** (the stars and best moves for each level, a level you're part-way through, and your settings, with your answer about statistics), a count of the levels finished since the last full-screen ad with the time of the last ad (so the game can keep ads rare), and for the reminders the time you last had the game open and which messages have shown (so they stay quiet while you play and don't repeat), are saved on your device only. Apart from the statistics above, which you can turn off, nothing about your play is sent to the developer.
- The game's save is left out of Android's device backups.
- Uninstalling the app, or clearing its storage in Android's settings, deletes all of it.

## Permissions

- **Vibration**, for short ticks when you tap, a mechanism clicks into place or you finish a level (you can turn it off in Settings).
- **Notifications**, for the reminders. On Android 13 and up the game asks first, after you finish your first level; you can say no, and turn them off later in Settings.
- **Run at startup**, so the reminders are set again after your phone restarts. The game doesn't start or run anything else then.
- **Internet and network state**, for the ads and the statistics.
- **Advertising ID**, for the ads, and for the statistics outside the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, as described above.
- **Install referrer**, which Google Analytics uses to learn where the game was installed from (such as the Play Store).
- Google's ads library also declares a few permissions of its own, which the game itself doesn't use: Android's ad services (the Privacy Sandbox's privacy-preserving ad features), local network access, and keeping a short background task running.
